## Ritual: stranded cap on upward scroll (root cause found and fixed)

The "Ritual plays twice when scrolling up" report traced to **one** defect in
`src/app/_components/three/useBottleUncap.ts`, in the master controller's Zone 3.

The cap's showcase close is a **timed** tween (`gsap.to(cap.position, …)`) chasing the scroll
position across 3.9 → 4.7 screens. Zone 3's upward branch only called
`gsap.killTweensOf(cap.position)` — which **freezes the cap wherever it happens to be**. So any
reversal inside that window left the cap stranded partway (or fully) lifted:

- reverse mid-glide → cap frozen ~60% up;
- reverse between 3.0 and 3.9, before the close starts → cap frozen fully lifted all the way up
  to the 2.5-screen reset.

That is scene 1 (detached cap, no mist). Scene 2 was the next downward pass re-running the whole
uncap + spray + steps sequence — the two together read as the scene "creating 2 times".

**Fix:** the upward branch now *seats* the cap at `baseCapY` instead of freezing it, guarded on
`!gsap.isTweening(cap.position)` so the tween starts once and is allowed to finish rather than
being restarted every wheel tick.

Also fixed while in there:

- Zone 3 announced `SPRAY_COMPLETE_EVENT` on **every** `onUpdate` once `closeProgress >= 1` — i.e.
  on every wheel tick between 4.7 and 5.0 screens, each one re-running `reveal()` in `<Ritual>`.
  Now guarded by a `hasAnnouncedClose` flag, reset in Zone 1 and Zone 2.
- `SHOWCASE_CLOSE_DURATION` was dead (left over from the rewrite — the close became a
  scroll-driven glide). Replaced with two named constants for the durations actually in use:
  `CLOSE_CHASE` (0.15) and `RESEAT_DURATION` (0.25). The file now lints with **zero** warnings.

Hand-traced the four passes (full down, full up, reverse mid-close, reverse before the close) and
the fix holds on each: no cap detach and no mist on the way up, and the open-bottle reading window
on the way down is untouched.

## Liquid colour did not match the button — `juiceColor` was applied twice

Reported: the 3D liquid reads grey-brown while the button (`--accent`) is a sage/olive, and the
button is the one that is right.

**Root cause: `juiceColor` ran twice on the same colour.** It is a channel subtraction, so it
compounds rather than being idempotent:

- `PersistentBottle.tsx:45` passes `liquidColor={juiceColor(variant.hex)}` — already treated.
- `BottleScene.tsx` then did `const juice = juiceColor(accent)` on that same prop.

For Parada (`hex #b7bb81` → `readableAccent` `#7c7f56` → first pass `#465000`) the second pass
produced **`#262100`**, a near-black brown with no green left. At the liquid's half opacity over
the parchment that composited to a grey-taupe — exactly what the screenshot shows.

Measured from the screenshot and from the maths:

| | hue | sat | val |
|---|---|---|---|
| `#7c7f56` button `--accent` (target) | 64° | 32% | 50% |
| `#a7a59a` liquid before (sampled from the PNG) | 51° | **8%** | 65% |
| `#9d9e70` liquid after (predicted) | 61° | **29%** | 62% |

**Fix** — `BottleScene.tsx` now treats the prop as already-treated and only juices the raw
`--accent` token fallback:

```ts
const juice = liquidColor ?? juiceColor(readCssToken("--accent", "#b87333"));
```

Also corrected the `liquidColor` prop doc, which claimed the raw hex was acceptable.

### The convention (now consistent)

Callers pass a **`juiceColor(...)` result**, never the raw hex — that is what `BottlePreview`
documents and what `PersistentBottle` and `MegaMenu` both do. `BottleGltf` does no treatment of
its own, so the transform happens exactly once per path.

### Latent problems found, NOT fixed (need a decision)

- **`juiceColor`'s generic branch destroys hue.** It subtracts a flat 30/36/36 per channel and
  scales each by a different factor (0.96 / 0.76 / 0.78), which crushes G and B harder than R and
  skews every hue toward brown. Sage `#7a7e68` → `#584435`. Only the two hard-coded catalogue
  branches save Parada and Boom Shell.
- **Those branches match on a magic string.** They key on `readableAccent(hex)`, so they fire only
  while a fragrance's *darkened* accent happens to equal `#7c7f56` or `#9a7177`. Any new SKU whose
  `readableAccent` collides silently inherits another fragrance's liquid colour.
- `src/app/_components/three/bottle-materials.ts` is **dead** — nothing imports it, and its
  transmissive `LIQUID_MATERIAL` describes an approach the live code deliberately does not use
  (nothing refracts; see `GLASS_MATERIAL` in `BottleGltf`).
- `BottleScene.tsx` carries 5 unused imports (`AgXToneMapping` is used, but `ACESFilmicToneMapping`,
  `Box3`, `Vector3`, `Object3D`, `useScrollScene` are not) and 3 `react-hooks/refs` **errors**
  from passing the `refs` bundle as a prop. All pre-existing, all from the rewrite; left alone to
  avoid churning a file the user may still be refactoring.

## Bottle realism pass — the glass rim and the liquid

Reported: the vessel "prickles in the eyes" and does not look real. Structure and design to stay
untouched, so this is all materials and shaders in `BottleGltf.tsx`.

### Why it glared (diagnosed, not guessed)

`applyGlassEdge` compounded two exponents: `pow(1 - cosθ, 2.5)` fed into `pow(fresnel, 2.2)` is an
effective **5.5**, which squeezes the highlight into the last pixel or two of the contour. That
tight, it stops reading as light on a curved surface and reads as a stroke drawn round the shape.
On top of that the glint was added as unattenuated `vec3(glint)` and the alpha ceiling was `0.85`
on a body that is only 8% opaque — a hard white edge on a nearly invisible fill. The cap sleeve was
the worst of it at `glintStrength 0.95 / alphaGlint 0.60`.

**First attempt over-corrected and made the vessel nearly invisible.** Softening the falloff *and*
halving the strengths was two reductions at once: the alpha ceiling went `0.85 → 0.42`, so the glass
lost its silhouette entirely against the parchment. An 8%-opaque white shell on cream is very
nearly nothing — **the rim is the only thing that makes the vessel visible**, so it has to be
strong; the trick is being strong without glaring.

**Corrected values** (v10): one exponent `3.0`, warm glint, ceiling `0.62` — the middle between the
glaring `0.85` and the invisible `0.42`. Bottle `0.40 / 0.44 / 0.20`, cap `0.46 / 0.46 / 0.26`,
glass body opacity `0.08 → 0.10`.

Also made the rim **directional**, which is the other half of why an even ring reads as drawn:
`lit = smoothstep(-0.35, 0.85, dot(N, keyDir))` weights the glint toward the lit side
(`mix(0.30, 1.0, lit)`) and the contour toward the shadow side (`mix(1.0, 0.55, lit)`), so the two
together describe a solid rather than a shape. It reads the live normal, so it follows the variant
spin and the showcase tilt.

### The lights had been rewritten — worth knowing

`<BottleScene>` is no longer the three lights it was: it now has **six directional lights plus
`ambientLight 0.5`**, all low-intensity (0.18–0.30) with the env map doing most of the work. The key
is the **only warm-tinted and brightest** one, `[3.2, 2.4, 4.2]` at `0.30` in `#fffaf2` — which is
what `keyDir` is normalised from. My first `keyDir` was built from a stale read of the old lights;
a check that derives the direction from `BottleScene.tsx` and compares caught it, and will catch it
again if the lighting moves.

### Why it looked flat

The liquid was a uniform `opacity 0.3` fill with `clearcoat: 0` — a single flat tone, which the eye
reads as paint. Added the two cues a real fill has, injected into the fragment shader at
`dithering_fragment`:

- **Beer-Lambert depth** — a vertical gradient over the mesh's own extent, squared so it stays
  gentle through the middle and only bites near the bottom.
- **A meniscus** — a bright band at the top of the column where surface tension pulls the liquid up
  the glass and the curve catches the key light. The strongest single cue that the top of the fill
  is a surface.

Plus `clearcoat 0.35` on the liquid so the surface carries its own specular. The body range is
measured from `liquid.geometry.boundingBox` and passed as uniforms rather than hardcoded, and the
gradient keys off the *undeformed* `position.y` via a new `vBodyY` varying, so it stays anchored to
the glass while the surface sloshes.

**Dip tube:** was `opacity 0.80` pure white — a hard bright line laid over the bottle. Now `0.32`
and warm off-white (`#e6e0d4`).

### Verified against the model

Parsed `public/perfume_bottle.glb` directly (header → JSON chunk) rather than assuming:

- liquid POSITION bounds are `y -1.0 … 1.0`, so the top of the geometry *is* the surface the
  meniscus should sit on;
- **no material is shared between nodes** (`capDarkMaterial.001`, `pipeMaterial.001`,
  `perfumeLiquidMaterial` etc. are all distinct), so mutating one cannot bleed into another;
- cap ships as `baseColor [0,0,0]`, metallic 0.3, roughness 0.25 — left alone deliberately, since a
  clearcoat on pure black would put a hard white streak on it, which is the glare being removed.

GLSL checked structurally (8/8): `vBodyY` declared in *both* shaders and written in `begin_vertex`,
both uniforms declared and bound, all four injected blocks brace-balanced, call site passing the
measured range, and both `customProgramCacheKey`s bumped so a stale program is not reused.

### "Liquid glass" — the rim rebuilt around the reference (v12)

User supplied Apple's *Liquid Glass* as the target: a clear middle, an edge that gathers and splits
light, no hard line. Rebuilt `applyGlassEdge` into four layers: a **broad halo** (visibility), a
**thin chromatic contour** (three stepped exponents 4.6/5.6/6.6 put red widest and blue narrowest, so
the edge warms as it gathers), an **inner shade** (thickness), and an **alpha gather** (ceiling 0.66).
Dispersion is directional — warm where the key hits, cool opposite.

**The finding that matters, and it took a simulation to see it.** On a *light* page you cannot make
glass visible by adding light. The shell is a semi-transparent white layer over cream, so additive
brightness cancels against the backdrop when it composites. The only cue with real contrast is the
**darkening** — which means alpha has to be high enough in the band for that darkening to survive.
That is why every previous attempt either glared or vanished.

Simulated the composited contrast (Δ vs `#f3ece0`) across grazing angle before choosing values:

| grazing | 0.65 | 0.80 | 0.90 | 0.97 | 1.00 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| original bottle | 3 | 12 | **26** | **41** | **49** |
| original cap | 15 | **47** | **91** | **130** | **138** |
| v9 (too faint) | 0 | 1 | 1 | 0 | 1 |
| **v12 bottle** | 6 | **12** | **14** | 7 | 2 |
| **v12 cap** | 8 | **16** | **17** | 7 | 6 |

The original was a *spike* — Δ138 is essentially pure white on cream, which is exactly the
"prickling". v9 was flat zero. v12 is a **broad band peaking at 14–17 that returns to neutral at the
silhouette**, which is the lens read: a soft ring of thickness, no line.

Shipped values: bottle `(rim 0.24, halo 0.16, gather 0.60, inner 0.50)` body `0.10`; cap
`(0.28, 0.20, 0.50, 0.54)` body `0.26`. The cap is deliberately denser — it is a thicker sleeve, and
the reference shows it as a distinct glass ring — so its expectations differ from the wall's.

## Ritual: the three cap events now run in sequence, not at once

Reported: on very fast scrolling, cap opening / mist revealing / cap closing conflict.

**Cause.** The three are a *sequence*, but only the first two are timed together — uncapping and
spraying share one clock. The third is placed by **scroll position**. So at walking pace the two
agree, and on a fast pass they do not: the customer crosses the close window (3.9 screens) while the
mist is still in the air (~1.35s from the trigger), and the controller took that as an instruction.
The cap glided shut **over a spraying nozzle**, and at 4.7 it `kill()`ed `activeSprayTl` outright —
truncating the mist. Three things the eye reads as one gesture, all happening at once.

**Fix — scroll now only *requests* the close.** Two new flags in `useBottleUncap`:

- `sprayDone` — set on the spray timeline's `onComplete` (and immediately on the reduced-motion /
  no-mesh path). **The cap may not travel back down before this.**
- `closeRequested` — set when scroll enters the close window. Remembered rather than acted on, so a
  fast pass still gets its close afterwards instead of losing it.

`settleAfterSpray()` is the bridge: called from `onComplete`, it applies whatever the scroll asked
for while the mist was up — `applyClose()` if a close was pending, else `seatCap()` if the customer
was heading back up. Without it a fast pass would be left with a permanently open cap, because the
scroll had already moved on and no further `onUpdate` would come.

The same gate covers the reverse: the upward branch seats the cap only when `sprayDone`, since a cap
coming down over a live spray is the same conflict seen backwards. The close window is now named
`CLOSE_FROM` / `CLOSE_TO` rather than repeated as literals in two places.

**Verified 16/16** by modelling the controller's decision ladder and driving it with scroll
scenarios, asserting the ordering rather than the arithmetic:

- fast pass (whole Ritual in 0.4s): order is `uncap -> spray-complete -> close`, spray never killed
- slow pass: unchanged — the case that already worked must not regress
- stop mid-spray at 4.2: the close is still applied once the mist lands, not lost
- reverse out mid-spray: the cap waits, then seats, and the spray runs to completion
- fast upward pass: only a reset, no second uncap

The simulation first failed scenario 1 and it was the *test* that was wrong — it asserted
immediately after the scroll, before the spray's own 1.35s clock had run. Worth remembering: the
spray finishes after the customer has stopped scrolling.

## The Ritual journey is now paced by its own clock (scroll-jack, Ritual window only)

Requested: however fast the customer scrolls, the whole journey — cap opening, mist, cap closing,
bottle rotating — must play through, and never conflict. The user chose the pacing model
explicitly: **hold only the Ritual window**, leaving the Hero and Manifesto free.

`useBottleUncap` now runs on **two clocks**, and keeping them apart is the whole design:

- **the page** — the customer's, free through the Hero and Manifesto, however hard they flick;
- **the journey** — the sequence's own, which can only advance at `MAX_JOURNEY_RATE`.

Every zone reads the journey, never the page, so the thresholds at 3.0 / 3.9 / 4.7 are always
crossed in order and can never be jumped. `MAX_JOURNEY_RATE` is **derived, not chosen**:
`(CLOSE_TO - RITUAL_FROM) / SPRAY_TOTAL` = 1.7 screens / 1.35s = **1.259 screens/s** — the rate at
which the window and the mist finish together.

### Three traps the simulation caught, all of which I had walked into

1. **The deadlock.** The obvious hold clamps the page to the journey — but the journey chased the
   page, and the ScrollTrigger reports the *clamped* position. So the clamp told the journey "you
   are already where you wanted to be", the distance read zero, and the journey **stopped dead with
   the cap half open**. Fixed by chasing `intentScreen` instead: the furthest the page has been
   seen to reach while heading down (the position itself while heading back). The customer's wheel
   still says "five screens"; the journey takes its own time getting there.

2. **The yank.** Clamping the page *back* to the journey drags it backwards by however far it
   overshot — measured at **2.0 screens** in simulation, at exactly the moment the customer was
   scrolling hardest. Fixed with `holdAnchor`: the page is held *at* where it already is
   (`max(journey, anchor)`) and the journey comes up to meet it. The page now never steps backwards
   at all — asserted as a hard invariant.

3. **The skipped window.** A single tick could carry the journey from below `RITUAL_ENTRY` to past
   `CLOSE_TO`, and since `limiting` is read *before* the step that tick ran at full speed and
   skipped the whole sequence. The step is now clamped so it can never overshoot the entrance.

Also: the limit applies **forward only, inside the window only**. Below `RITUAL_ENTRY` the journey
tracks the page exactly (rate-limiting from zero would leave it lagging the Hero, and the hold
would then have to drag the page back to it); reversing is unlimited, because braking a customer
who is leaving only feels like lag.

### Verified 19/19

Modelling the pacing and asserting the *output* page position, not the target gap: hard flick (full
journey in order, 1.37s, **zero** backwards steps), slow scroll (never held), intro (free, reaches
2.5 unimpeded), reverse (no brake, reset fires), stop mid-window (cap waits for the mist, then
closes), gentle-then-flick (the intent is not forgotten).

### Worth remembering about the tooling

A `sed -i '' -e 'N,Md'` invocation on macOS **silently mangled the file** — the first form errored
without applying, the second deleted the wrong range, and I only noticed because a later line count
came back 537 instead of 703. Recovered from the `cp` backup I had taken first. Bulk line-range
deletion is safer through a node script that *verifies the boundary lines before writing* and
refuses if they do not match — which is how the redo was done.

## Scrollbar hidden (globals.css)

`html, body { scrollbar-width: none; -ms-overflow-style: none }` plus `::-webkit-scrollbar
{ display: none }` on both, in the `@layer base`. Three declarations because there is no one way to
say it — standard (Firefox / Chromium 121+), WebKit, and legacy Edge. Only the chrome is removed;
the document still scrolls and Lenis, the wheel and every ScrollTrigger are untouched.

Named on both `html` and `body` because which is the scrolling element depends on the document's own
overflow, and `<Navigation>` toggles that on both while a panel is open.

Side benefit: that same toggle takes the scrollbar's gutter away, so on any platform with
classic (space-taking) scrollbars the page used to shift sideways by its width whenever the cart or
menu opened. With the scrollbar never occupying space there is nothing to take away.

Verified against the compiled stylesheet served by the dev server, not just the source.

**Still visible:** the four internal scroll containers keep theirs — `MegaMenu`, `MobileMenu`,
`CartDrawer` and the Ritual panel in `OpeningStage` (mobile only). On macOS these are overlay
scrollbars that only appear mid-scroll; on Windows/Linux they are persistent. Not hidden because the
ask was the page scrollbar; say the word to extend it.

### Follow-up: the hold was not smooth on a fast scroll

Reported after shipping: "as i scroll soo fast the restricted scroll is not smooth."

**The hold was *correcting* the page, not driving it.** It set the scroll only once the page had
run more than a pixel ahead (`if (window.scrollY > allowed + 1)`). So the customer's wheel pushed
the page forward, the hold snapped it back, and the two alternated frame by frame — a sawtooth whose
amplitude grew with scroll speed, which is exactly why it was worse the faster they scrolled.

Why it could not simply be driven: driving the page to the journey makes the ScrollTrigger report
the journey, so a journey chasing it reads a distance of zero. **The intent has to come from
somewhere the hold does not write.**

**The redesign** — two modes in the tick, only ever one of them:

- **Holding** — latched. `holdTo` is the end of the sequence, and the journey runs to it at
  `MAX_JOURNEY_RATE` while the page is driven to wherever the journey has got to, *every* frame.
- **Free** — the journey follows the page, capped at the rate inside the Ritual. This is where a
  fast page is caught: once it has genuinely outrun the journey by `LATCH_GAP` (0.12 screens), the
  hold is taken.

Two things this got wrong first, both caught by simulation:

1. **The destination cannot be read at the latch.** The obvious `holdTo = the customer's ask` gives
   a destination one frame of scroll past the journey, and the journey stops a third of the way in
   (the sequence never completes). It is always `CLOSE_TO` — the hold exists to play the Ritual for
   someone who scrolled past it, so the end of the sequence *is* the destination.
2. **Lenis has to be frozen in the same breath as the latch.** Otherwise it keeps easing toward the
   target it was given, moves the page on before the next tick can place it, and the first frame of
   the hold shows as a step backwards. `setScroll` at the latch point is what stops it.

**Verified 14/14**, measuring the *rendered* position (end-of-tick, which is what the browser paints)
rather than the within-tick correction, which never reaches the screen: hard flick and long fast
scroll both complete the sequence with **zero** backwards steps and **zero** direction reversals;
slow scroll is never held; reverse fires its reset.

Worth remembering for next time: the within-tick correction is invisible — the browser paints once,
after GSAP's ticker has run both Lenis's `raf` and this tick. Measuring it as if it were rendered
produced a false failure.

## Nozzle turned to the front, mist aimed at the viewer

Reported: the pump's nozzle points sideways, and the mist should come out of it toward the customer.

**Where the spout actually points, from the asset.** Parsed `perfume_bottle.glb`: the `nozzle` node is
a child of `automizerButton` at `translation [0.151, 0.320, -1.134]`, and the orifice is the
`sphereMaterial.001` primitive spanning `z[-1.158..-0.794]` of that button. So the spout runs along
the model's own **−Z**.

The wrapper group's yaw was `-PI/2`, chosen for the dip tube's curve — which mapped that spout to
world **+X: hard right**, a full quarter turn off front. That is the bug, and it is also why the
plume's axis was `[0.945, 0.12, 0.12]`: the mist *was* aligned with the spout, just with a spout
pointing the wrong way.

**Fix**

- `BottleGltf` — new `NOZZLE_YAW = PI + atan2(0.151, 1.134)` = 187.58°, replacing `-Math.PI / 2`.
  The `PI` turns the spout to the front; the `atan2` trim takes out the 7.6° the nozzle's own
  sideways offset leaves in it, so the spout lands on **+Z exactly** (verified: direction
  `(0.000, 0.000, 1.000)`, x = 0.00005). Cost: the dip tube's curve is now on the other side.
- `BottleMist` — `PLUME.axis` `[0.945, 0.12, 0.12]` → `[0, 0.12, 0.99]`: fires along the new spout
  at the viewer, with a deliberate 6.9° upward arc. No X component, because the yaw trim already
  removed the spout's sideways lean.
- `BottleMist` — the spread basis moved from `(0,0,1) × axis` to `(0,1,0) × axis`. The old reference
  is **degenerate once the plume fires along +Z** — the cross product collapses and the cone comes
  out as a line. This would have been an easy one to miss.
- `DEFAULT_NOZZLE` retuned to the mirrored position; it only paints the first frame, and the
  material starts at `opacity: 0`, so it is never seen — the live position is locked to the nozzle
  node every frame.

The tilt choreography holds the vessel **upright (0,0,0) through the whole Ritual spray window**
(2.0 → 4.5 screens), so the yaw alone decides the nozzle's direction where it matters. The Manifesto
lean (−8°) and the showcase pose (y 0.35) tilt it slightly off front, and the variant spin sweeps it
round and back — all transient.

**Verified 8/8** against the asset and the source: the old yaw sent the spout right, the new one
lands on +Z with no left/right lean, the plume's horizontal direction matches the spout to 0.00°,
its arc is upward, the spread basis is not degenerate, and the mist is aimed at the viewer.

## Near-clear fragrances render as clear liquid, not grey

Reported: Cobalt Elixir is almost white but the app shows it grey.

**Cause — the near-clear case had no handling at all.** `juiceColor` ran every colour through
`readableAccent` (which floors luminance to 0.2 for text legibility) and then a channel-wise
darkening. Cobalt Elixir is `#e5e5e5`, a neutral near-white, and came out **`#5a4345`**: dark *and*
warm, a cast its own hex does not contain, because the calibration scales each channel by a
different factor (0.96 / 0.76 / 0.78). Over the parchment that rendered `#c3b8b6` — the grey.

The design intent was already written down in the file: pale juices get their own faint cast
amplified and are otherwise left pale. But the code for it had been dropped by an earlier rewrite,
leaving `paleFactor` and `LIQUID_TINT_BOOST` **dead** — documented, unused, and the only two lint
warnings in the file. Nothing was handling the pale end.

**Fix** — a `paleJuice` route, taken when the fragrance's own `relativeLuminance` is at or above
`LIQUID_LUM_DENSE` (0.6): keep the juice's own luminance and amplify only its deviation from its own
grey level, ramping to `LIQUID_TINT_BOOST`× at the clear end. A neutral near-white stays exactly
itself; a faintly tinted one gains a cast.

Measured, with the four other fragrances **byte-identical** before and after:

| fragrance | hex | was | now |
|---|---|---|---|
| Cobalt Elixir | `#e5e5e5` | `#5a4345` (lum 0.066, chroma 0.256) | **`#e5e5e5`** (lum 0.784, chroma 0.000) |
| Boom Shell | `#e5aab2` | `#7c2e3c` | `#7c2e3c` |
| Parada | `#b7bb81` | `#465000` | `#465000` |
| Midnight Oud | `#bb7528` | `#8a3701` | `#8a3701` |
| Midnight Sapphire | `#323b8f` | `#131153` | `#131153` |

`paleFactor` is 0 for all four others, so the switch is tight: only the near-clear SKU changes.

**Verified 9/9** against the real module under `--experimental-strip-types`: Cobalt Elixir is
near-white and perfectly neutral, the four others unchanged, a tinted near-white (`#f0e8e6`) gains a
cast (chroma 0.042 → 0.100), and pure white stays pure white.

Note the *button* is still grey for Cobalt Elixir, and correctly so — `readableAccent` floors it for
legibility on the parchment, and a near-white button on cream would be invisible. Liquid and accent
are allowed to disagree at the pale end, and that is the point of handling them separately.
